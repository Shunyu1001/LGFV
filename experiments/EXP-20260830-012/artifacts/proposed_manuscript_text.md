# Proposed manuscript text

## Sample selection

The historical-capacity sample contains 84 human-reviewed cases, including 12
cases coded as substantive exit or functional transfer. The complete-control
sample retains 78 cases and all 12 institutional-change outcomes. The six
excluded cases are all nominal exits, however, and their mean historical elite
density is -0.726 standard deviations in the matched sample, compared with
0.056 among the included cases. We therefore treat the complete-control
comparison as a descriptive diagnostic rather than a neutral adjustment for
contemporary conditions, and we do not impute the missing controls.

## Sparse-outcome estimates

We report four fixed models without selecting among them by coefficient sign
or statistical significance. In the 84-case sample, a one-standard-deviation
increase in elite density is associated with a 6.6-percentage-point change in
the probability of substantive exit or functional transfer under the linear
probability model and a 5.6-percentage-point average marginal effect under
Firth logit. The corresponding 95 percent diagnostic intervals are -1.3 to
14.6 and -0.8 to 11.9 percentage points. These estimates remain exploratory
and associational.

For the 78-case complete-control sample, we use district/county platforms as
the reference category and retain a prefecture/municipal indicator, which
removes the exact alias in the previous two-indicator construction. The
corrected design has eight independent columns but only 12 events. The linear
probability and Firth-logit effects are 0.5 and 0.3 percentage points, with 95
percent diagnostic intervals of -8.3 to 9.4 and -9.8 to 10.5 percentage
points. These adjusted estimates are not stable evidence of a historical-
capacity association.

## Influence and fixed effects

The historical-only effects retain their sign under every case deletion: the
linear probability estimate ranges from 4.8 to 8.2 percentage points, while
the Firth-logit average marginal effect ranges from 4.3 to 6.7 percentage
points. By contrast, the adjusted linear probability estimate ranges from
-0.9 to 2.3 percentage points and changes sign in 9 of 78 deletions, while the
adjusted Firth-logit estimate ranges from -1.0 to 1.8 percentage points and
changes sign in 11 deletions. We do not estimate province fixed effects because
the candidate design would contain 25 independent columns for 12 events and
only 4 of 18 provinces have within-province outcome variation.

## Placement recommendation

Retain the historical-only linear probability model as an exploratory
descriptive baseline and, if space permits, report Firth logit as a fixed
rare-outcome sensitivity diagnostic. Move the adjusted models, selection
comparison, and influence results to the appendix or remove the adjusted
coefficient from the paper's evidentiary claims. This recommendation follows
from event support, complete-case selection, and deletion stability rather
than the direction or statistical significance of an estimate.
